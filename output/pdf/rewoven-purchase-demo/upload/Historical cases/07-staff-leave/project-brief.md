# Staff Leave Approval Tool - Project brief

Fictional demonstration material. All organizations, people, events, and results are invented.

**Delivery company:** Threadline Delivery  
**Client:** Lark Studio (fictional)  
**Project:** Staff Leave Approval Tool  
**Period:** April 2026  
**Status:** Delivered manager delegation pilot  
**Document version:** 1

## Purpose and problem

Threadline Delivery built leave-request review with time-bounded manager delegation. It recorded the acting reviewer and the manager on whose behalf a decision was made.

Leave requests waited when a manager was unavailable. Staff needed a named temporary reviewer instead of sharing manager credentials or treating an email reply as permanent permission. The team needed a way to explain who acted and why that person had authority at the time of the decision. This was a staffing workflow, with no financial spending limits.

## People and responsibilities

An employee requested leave. Their manager could approve or reject it. An administrator could register a temporary delegate for a specified manager and period. The delegate could act only within that active assignment. The project did not decide staffing policy, approve payroll changes, or treat a delegate as a permanent replacement manager.

## Agreed workflow

A leave request moved from submitted to reviewed. When a delegate reviewed it, the server checked the delegation at decision time and recorded both actor and represented manager. Expired delegation could not authorize another decision. A manager returning from leave could still inspect earlier decisions, with the original actor retained rather than rewritten as if the manager had clicked the button.

## Boundaries and constraints

The pilot used existing staff identities and a manually maintained manager map. There was a single managerial review stage. Financial amount thresholds, separation of manager and finance duties, purchase revisions, and supplier records were outside scope. A colleague being an appropriate leave delegate did not establish that the same person could authorize spending.
