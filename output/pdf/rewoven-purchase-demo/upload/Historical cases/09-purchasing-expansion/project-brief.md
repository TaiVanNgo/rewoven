# Purchasing Expansion Proposal - Project brief

Fictional demonstration material. All organizations, people, events, and results are invented.

**Delivery company:** Threadline Delivery  
**Client:** Meadow Office Services (fictional)  
**Project:** Purchasing Expansion Proposal  
**Period:** June 2026  
**Status:** Proposal only; not implemented  
**Document version:** 1

## Purpose and problem

Threadline Delivery proposed a purchasing expansion with amount-based finance review. The client paused the proposal before implementation, so it supplies design discussion but no delivered precedent or achieved outcome.

After its one-office purchasing pilot, Meadow considered expansion to additional offices. The proposal described manager approval followed by finance review above a configurable threshold. The client had not agreed the threshold, finance assignment ownership, or handling of edits between stages. These were questions for discovery, not rules already operating in a delivered system.

## People and responsibilities

The proposed users were office requesters, assigned managers, finance reviewers, and an administrator maintaining assignments. The proposal described responsibilities but did not provision real roles or grant authority. A person appearing in a design diagram was not evidence of an implemented permission check. The original one-office pilot remained the only delivered purchasing workflow in this client history.

## Agreed workflow

The design sketch moved a submitted request to manager review and, where required by a future policy, then to finance review. It proposed binding both decisions to a single request revision. An edited amount would invalidate earlier decisions. The diagram contained unresolved branches for delegation, absence of a finance reviewer, and the meaning of a rejection at the second stage.

## Boundaries and constraints

The document was a discovery proposal prepared before funding approval. No sprint was authorized, no migration was applied, and no acceptance environment was created. A four-week delivery estimate in an early email was explicitly labelled preliminary and contingent on policy decisions. It was not a record of completed work, a reliable estimate for a different client, or evidence of improved approval speed.

## How to read this brief

This brief records the agreed scope or, where explicitly marked, the proposed scope. It is not by itself a delivery certificate. The implementation notes explain decisions and examples; the separate delivery review records what was actually delivered, tested, paused, or left unresolved. Read those documents together before describing a requirement as completed work.
